FINAL REVISED NARRATION

After a lifetime of struggle and eventual success, Charles Bukowski left two words on his tombstone: 'Don't try.' He faced 30 years of rejection. His breakthrough at 50 seemed like the ultimate underdog story. But his true success came from accepting who he was, not from wanting it more. This is 'The Backwards Law,' a concept from philosopher Alan Watts. The harder you chase good feelings, the more they elude you. Straining for success makes you feel like a failure. The author found bold moves happened when he stopped obsessing over outcomes. Accept discomfort, and it loses its power. What if you just let yourself feel it?
