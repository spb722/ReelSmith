# Instagram caption

The caption sits under the reel. Its job: make someone tap "…more", tell
Instagram's search what the reel is about, and ask for a save, a share and
a comment.

## Hard limits (checked in code; a caption that breaks one is rejected)

- The hook line is at most 125 characters. Only about the first 125
  characters show before "…more", and most people read nothing else.
- The whole caption is at most 2,200 characters.
- Exactly 5 hashtags: 4 niche tags about the topic, then `#bookreels` last.
  Lowercase letters, digits and underscores only, no spaces, never repeated.
- 3-5 search phrases, and every one of them appears word for word in the
  caption text (hook, concept, story, turn, action, save/share or question).
- The question ends with a question mark.
- No markdown. Instagram shows `**bold**` and `_italics_` as literal
  symbols, so never use them.

## Start from the reel's own words

- Write from the supplied narration, not from memory of the book. Don't
  add facts, numbers or quotes the reel doesn't contain.
- The caption should reuse the reel's words. Instagram also reads the words
  spoken and shown on screen, so matching them strengthens the search signal.
- If the reel says a story "may not have really happened", the caption
  says so too.
- Only credit a book or author when the supplied source text names them.
  Otherwise leave credit out.

## The parts, in order

The code joins your parts in this order, with a blank line between each,
and adds the ✅, 📌 and 💬 signposts and the hashtag line itself. Don't
write those three emojis, and don't put hashtags anywhere in the text.

1. **hook** - one line. Reuse the reel's opening hook. Open with its most
   concrete, surprising detail (a price, a number, a scene), not a general
   statement. End with one emoji.
2. **concept** - 1-2 lines. Say the key idea by name, e.g. "That's
   pre-crastination." People search for names.
3. **story** - 2-4 short lines (one list item per line). The example or
   study from the reel, in plain words. At most one emoji in the section.
4. **turn** - 1-2 lines. The main takeaway, said plainly.
5. **action** - one small thing the viewer can do today.
6. **save_share** - one save ask and one share ask, each made specific:
   "Save this for your next 'busy but nothing done' day, and send it to the
   friend who clears their inbox first thing." Never just "save this".
7. **question** - a specific question people can answer in a few words.
   Reuse or adapt the reel's closing question, so the reel and the caption
   finish on the same note. Beats a vague "thoughts?".

## Writing rules

- Write 3-5 phrases people might type into search (e.g. "time management",
  "procrastination", "to-do list") naturally into the sentences, and list
  them in `search_phrases`. Don't stuff them in.
- Short lines and lots of white space. People read captions on phones, so
  keep each line to 1-2 sentences.
- Few emojis, used as signposts: about one per section, never a string.
- Warm and plain, like the narration. No clickbait, no hype words.

## Hashtags

- Pick specific tags that say what the reel is about (`#precrastination`,
  `#timemanagement`) over broad ones (`#motivation`, `#love`,
  `#instagood`), which add little.
- Never use a tag unrelated to the reel.

## Revising

When you're given the current caption and feedback, change what the
feedback asks for and keep the rest. The current caption may have been
edited by hand, so treat its wording as the operator's choice. Still return
every part, and the hard limits still apply.

## Worked example

Parts:

- hook: `12 emails. 3 errands. 1 dentist booking. And the project that mattered? Untouched. 😮‍💨`
- concept: `That's not laziness. It's pre-crastination: the urge to start anything right away, even when it costs you more.`
- story:
  - `In one study, people had to carry one of two heavy buckets down a hallway.`
  - `One bucket sat right beside them. The other sat much closer to the finish, so it was the easier carry.`
  - `Almost everyone grabbed the near one and lugged it the whole way, just to start a few seconds sooner. 🪣`
- turn: `Your to-do list works the same way. You count tasks instead of weighing them. Being busy is not the same as getting things done.`
- action: `Before you start tomorrow, ask: what am I actually trying to win today?`
- save_share: `Save this for your next "busy but nothing done" day, and send it to the friend who clears their inbox first thing.`
- question: `What's the easy task you always grab first?`
- hashtags: `#productivitytips #precrastination #timemanagement #deepwork #bookreels`
- search_phrases: `pre-crastination`, `to-do list`, `getting things done`

The joined caption:

```
12 emails. 3 errands. 1 dentist booking. And the project that mattered? Untouched. 😮‍💨

That's not laziness. It's pre-crastination: the urge to start anything right away, even when it costs you more.

In one study, people had to carry one of two heavy buckets down a hallway.
One bucket sat right beside them. The other sat much closer to the finish, so it was the easier carry.
Almost everyone grabbed the near one and lugged it the whole way, just to start a few seconds sooner. 🪣

Your to-do list works the same way. You count tasks instead of weighing them. Being busy is not the same as getting things done.

✅ Before you start tomorrow, ask: what am I actually trying to win today?

📌 Save this for your next "busy but nothing done" day, and send it to the friend who clears their inbox first thing.

💬 What's the easy task you always grab first?

#productivitytips #precrastination #timemanagement #deepwork #bookreels
```
